WorldWeave: Rethinking Embodied Navigation through Cross-Task World Modeling

[EN] Mobile embodied agents continuously alter their observations through their own motion, while external dynamics further shape future interactions. Different mobile embodied tasks therefore capture complementary patterns of state evolution, yet jointly learning world dynamics across such interactions remains less explored. We investigate this problem through Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT), which respectively involve navigation toward a stationary goal and interaction with a moving target. Instead of conditioning state transitions on heterogeneous physical actions, WorldWeave learns latent actions from observed state changes via inverse-forward dynamics, establishing a shared transition representation across tasks. Tracking Objects Through Assembly and Decomposition

通过组装和分解跟踪对象
arXiv: 2611.26269 · 2026-11-19 · Cheryl Lam, Yihong Sun, Bharath Hariharan
[EN] Tracking objects through transformations is essential for understanding real-world dynamics. Recent work has extended traditional object tracking to detect state changes. However, existing methods require an initial object prompt and detect only its decompositions, missing assembly events, which begin with multiple objects rather than one. We therefore introduce a prompt-free formulation of this task, where the goal is to recover all object tracks and detect both decomposition and assembly events given only a video. Our key insight is that assembly is decomposition in reverse. Building on this insight, we present Atlas, a zero-shot system that constructs and jointly reasons over forward and reverse spatiotemporal partitions to recover object tracks and their transformations. We further introduce OmniST, a benchmark with dense annotations of objects and both transformation types. On OmniST, Atlas recovers more than twice as many state changes as prior methods while remaining competitive on existing benchmarks that only evaluate decomposition.

However, events reveal a target only through their spatial distribution, whose extent varies more than tenfold within one sequence. Event tracking therefore demands multi-scale representation, which existing spike-driven trackers lack. PPD treats the event counts cancelled by the voxel representation as privileged information. Its core constraint, Polarity-Guided Curvature Distillation (PGCD), aligns the curvature of the template-search relation under polarity-guided perturbations.
