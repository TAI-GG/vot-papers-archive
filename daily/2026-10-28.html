[EN] RGB-X tracking complements RGB appearance with thermal, depth, or event observations, but centralized training requires collecting multimodal videos at a single location. Federated training keeps raw videos local, but sensor heterogeneity can create a mismatch between parameter-sharing domains and the clients contributing to them. This mismatch is particularly relevant when RGB-only clients coexist with sensor-equipped clients: their RGB observations remain useful, but they provide no auxiliary measurements for learning sensor-dependent interactions. We propose Fed-MAPTrack, a modality-aware federated prompt tuning framework for RGB-X tracking. The pretrained tracking backbone and prediction head are frozen, and clients optimize and exchange only prompt-related parameters. The server aggregates RGB-shared parameters globally and maintains separate thermal, event, and depth banks for auxiliary-specific, fusion, and normalization parameters. Selective participation retains RGB-only clients&#39; contributions to the global RGB bank while excluding their fusion and normalization updates from modality-specific aggregation. Learning Direct Control from Optimization for Dynamic Target Tracking

2610.34929 · 2026-10-26 · Bing Sun, Yue Lin, Canhui Wen, Xin Chen, Yang Liu, Dong Wang
[EN] Dynamic target tracking requires a mobile robot to maintain target visibility while navigating obstacles with smooth, feasible controls. Online trajectory optimization explicitly encodes these objectives but requires repeated numerical solving, whereas waypoint-based neural policies separate geometric prediction from control execution. We introduce LECO, a framework that transfers tracking optimization into offline learning of a direct-control policy. LECO predicts control sequences and maps them to trajectories through a differentiable kinematic model, allowing trajectory-level objectives to directly guide control learning. A decaying expert-guidance curriculum initializes effective tracking behavior and progressively shifts learning toward these task objectives. At deployment, a predictive safety lock checks short-horizon obstacle clearance and invokes a classical planning and control fallback when the predicted motion is unsafe or the target prediction is invalid.
