[EN] Multi-object tracking in sports broadcasts is hard for two reasons: athletes move fast and change direction abruptly, and the camera pans and zooms at the same time. We present ARC-SORT (Adaptive, Robust, Compensated SORT), a tracking-by-detection tracker that trains nothing and runs in real time. Its design rule: the tracker measures its own uncertainty, and that measurement decides how it acts. An Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) Kalman filter gives every track a predictive covariance, and we turn it into a per-track search region: the less reliable the motion prediction, the larger the region in which a detection may match (uncertainty-adaptive Expansion IoU, EIoU). On identical inputs, this continuous coupling is what matters: the same covariance used as a hard Mahalanobis gate hurts at every threshold we swept, and richer covariance summaries add nothing measurable. When two tracks cross, the assignment cost itself reports whether the decision was close. [EN] Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) is an essential capability for intelligent robots in human-robot collaboration, requiring an agent to follow a designated target person in dynamic environments relying solely on egocentric visual perception. However, persistent pedestrian occlusions in dynamic scenes pose critical challenges to existing EVT methods. Lacking proactive visibility preservation, reactive tracking methods frequently experience prolonged target loss under severe occlusions. Therefore, we propose VP-EVT, a framework that treats target visibility as an explicit objective of motion control rather than a by-product of following. Acting on this objective requires understanding both what obstructs the target and how robot motion can change that obstruction. VP-EVT therefore uses language-guided perception to extract separate target and pedestrian observations. When occlusion makes current observations incomplete, VP-EVT combines visual history with ego-motion to maintain estimates of their relative positions and motion. Using these estimates and the current following waypoints, VP-EVT predicts whether continued following will sustain the obstruction. [EN] Markerless intraoperative tracking of spinal anatomy via RGB-D sensors is a promising alternative to invasive bone-anchored fiducials, yet no existing clinical dataset provides independent subsurface ground truth for validating these methods. We address this gap with a clinically viable pipeline that derives per-vertebra 6-DoF pose from routine intraoperative C-arm fluoroscopy, co-registered to the RGB-D camera frame through a multimodal fiducial array detectable in both imaging modalities. Critically, the pipeline requires no additional radiation, instrumentation, or deviation from standard surgical workflows, relying solely on the fluoroscopic images already acquired during routine level checks. A calibrated transform chain links the vertebral pose recovered via multi-view 2D-3D CT-to-fluoroscopy registration to the RGB-D coordinate frame, enabling direct comparison of any markerless registration estimate against a subsurface reference.
